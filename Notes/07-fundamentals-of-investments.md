**Source:** Dr Kanu Jain, *B.Com (Hons) III Year, VI Semester – Fundamentals of Investments, Week 2* (67 lecture slides). Read in full.

**Note:** despite the file name, this deck covers **technical analysis only**: assumptions, Dow theory, Elliott waves, chart types, patterns, gaps, indicators and limitations. It contains no fundamental analysis and no research findings. Examples are mostly Indian and US stocks (SBI, Axis Bank, P&G, Amazon) plus some FX (USD/CHF, GBP/USD).

## 1. What technical analysis (TA) is

- The study of **price charts** plus a few **momentum oscillators**.
- Based **entirely on price and volume history**. It ignores balance sheets and P&L accounts, which belong to fundamental analysis.
- Underlying belief: all price-sensitive information is already **built into the chart**. (The deck calls this "market efficiency". Strictly, the efficient-market hypothesis would say TA cannot beat the market; that tension is worth noticing.)
- Uses only **historical data**.

## 2. Three core assumptions

1. **The market discounts everything.** Price reflects all fundamentals, so only price needs to be studied.
2. **Prices move in trends.** Most TA strategies rest on this.
3. **History tends to repeat.** Market participants react consistently to similar stimuli, so patterns recur.

The extended list of "basic assumptions" adds:
- price is set by supply and demand;
- trends persist for long periods but **reversals** happen;
- charts can reveal **shifts** in supply and demand.

## 3. Dow Theory

- Based on **255 Wall Street Journal editorials** by **Charles H. Dow**, founder and first editor of the WSJ and co-founder of Dow Jones & Co.
- **Hypothesis:** the market is **not random**. It is driven by identifiable trends.
- **Three trends:**
  - **Primary trend:** the major move, lasting from under a year to several years. It can be bullish or bearish.
  - **Secondary trend:** an intermediate correction against the primary trend, lasting weeks to months. (The slide copies the primary-trend wording by mistake.)
  - **Minor trend:** day-to-day moves over a few days. Very short and noisy.
- Illustration: an upward primary trend, a downward primary trend and then a new upward primary trend, with secondary counter-moves inside each.
- An IYT (Dow Jones Transportation ETF) chart shows an up-channel with 20/50/200-day SMAs. In Dow theory the transports should confirm the industrials.

## 4. Elliott Wave Theory

- Developed by **Ralph Nelson Elliott**.
- Major long-term moves unfold in **five waves** in the direction of the trend: impulse waves 1–5, where waves 2 and 4 are corrections. A three-wave **A-B-C correction** follows.
- Patterns are **fractal**: each wave contains smaller waves (i–v, a–b–c).
- Applies to both **bull** and **bear** markets.
  - Bear example: a USD/CHF daily chart (2006) shows a 5-wave decline, then an A-B-C rally.
  - The A-B-C rally is measured with **Fibonacci levels** (23.6, 38.2, 50, 61.8, 100, 161.8, 261.8%).
- Real example (Axis Bank, 2014):
  - Waves: 0 = 369.05, 1 = 383.15, 2 = 371, 3 = 407.35, 4 = 390.11, 5 = 423.80.
  - Correction: A = 393.80, B = 412, C = 370.25.
  - Wave 3 extended to about **261%** of wave 1, wave 5 ended in a wedge, and C reached about **138%**.

## 5. Greater Fool Theory

- The belief that you can buy at **any price regardless of value** and still profit, because a "greater fool" will pay more later.
- This is the psychology of bubbles: doing something dumb while hoping someone else is dumber.

## 6. Chart types

- **Price data per period:** Open, High, Low, Close (OHLC).

| Chart | How it's built | Pros / cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Bar chart** | A vertical line from high to low. Open is a tick on the **left** and close a tick on the **right** (the slide says "horizontal dash") | Shows the full range; can include volume |
| **Line chart** | Joins one value (usually the close) over time | Clean trend view; **no** high/low/open information; can plot any variable (price, volume, index) |
| **Point & Figure** | Columns of **X** (rising) and **O** (falling); a new box only when price moves by a **chosen box size** | Ignores **time and volume**; filters noise; the analyst must define what counts as a "significant" move. Example: crude oil, intraday |
| **Candlestick** | A thin wick shows the range; the body runs from open to close | Colour-coded: **up period (close > open) = white/hollow**; **down period = red/black filled** |

- Candlestick worked example (SBI, March 2016):
  - 28 March: O 195.8, H 198.25, L 187.65, C 188.3. This is a large **black/bearish** body (−4.2%).
  - 30 March: O 192.25, C 197.55. This is a strong **white/bullish** body.
  - 24–25 March: O = H = L = C (196.6), a flat dash for days with no trading or a holiday.

## 7. Support and resistance

- **Support:** a price level where **demand** is strong enough to stop further falls. As price nears it and gets "cheaper", buyers step in and sellers hold back.
- **Resistance:** a price level where **supply/selling** is strong enough to stop further rises. As price nears it, sellers become eager and buyers reluctant.
- **Range strategy:** buy at support, sell at resistance (SMH example).
- Broken resistance often becomes **new support** ("resistance turned support"), and vice versa.

## 8. Chart patterns

### Classification
- **Reversal patterns** signal a change of trend and can be bullish or bearish:
  - head and shoulders (and inverse);
  - double tops and bottoms;
  - triple tops and bottoms;
  - rounding bottom.
- **Continuation patterns** signal a pause, after which the old trend resumes:
  - triangles;
  - rectangles;
  - flags;
  - pennants;
  - cup and handle.

### Head and shoulders (top): bearish reversal
- Forms at the **peak of an uptrend**: left shoulder, a higher head, a lower right shoulder, with a **neckline** under the troughs.
- **Sell signal = break below the neckline**, ideally confirmed by a **volume spike**.
- A **downward-sloping neckline** gives a **stronger** H&S top than an upward-sloping one (Home Depot vs GE examples).

### Inverse head and shoulders: bullish reversal
- Forms at the **end of a downtrend**. **Buy on the break above the neckline** (confirmation line).
- A **rising** confirmation line makes a stronger reversal than a falling one.
- Price often **retests** the broken neckline from above before moving on (gold futures example). This gives a second entry.

### Double top ("M") and double bottom ("W")
- Two attempts to continue the trend fail, and the trend reverses.
- **Double top:** found at the peak of an uptrend. Buyers are losing interest; the second high is often **lower** (rejected advance).
  - Sell when price breaks the confirmation line (the trough between the two highs), on a volume spike.
- **Double bottom:** price hits a low, rallies, falls back to the same area, then rises. It marks the end of a bearish trend.
  - Sequence (Pfizer example): first trough, peak, second trough, advance, **resistance break**, **resistance turned support**, price target.
- **Trading recipe** (strategies slide):

  | | Entry | Stop | Target |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Double top** | On the neckline break | Just above the neckline/right peak area | Neckline minus pattern height |
  | **Double bottom** | On the neckline break | Just below the neckline | Neckline plus pattern height |

### Rounding (saucer) bottom
- A **long-term** reversal, best on **weekly** charts.
- A long consolidation shifts from a bearish to a bullish bias. Breakout above the rim confirms the reversal.

### Cup and handle: bullish continuation
- After an advance, a bowl-shaped **cup** forms. A small sideways or down drift on the right forms the **handle**. A **breakout above the lip/handle range** signals continuation.
- Practical tip from the slide: **wait for the first bar to close above the lip** before entering.

### Triangles
- Each successive peak is lower and/or each successive trough is higher. The converging lines form a triangle.
- **Symmetric:** converging lines. A continuation pattern, usually with **declining volume** inside.
  - Measured target = height of the triangle's base added at the breakout (Sun Microsystems: 5.8 points).
- **Ascending (bullish):** flat top and rising lows. A breakout above the flat resistance, with a volume surge, is the signal (Walgreen).
- **Descending (bearish):** flat bottom and falling highs. A breakdown below flat support is the signal (DuPont).

### Flags
- A sharp rally or decline (the **flagpole**) is followed by a small **rectangle or parallelogram** consolidation, usually sloping against the trend. Price then continues in the original direction.
- Measured move = **flagpole length** projected from the breakout (HP: 10 points pole, then about 10-point continuation).
- Volume falls during the flag and rises on the breakout.

## 9. Gaps

- A **gap** is an open much higher or lower than the previous close.
- A gap **up** acts as **support**; a gap **down** acts as **resistance**. Assume the trend continues in the gap's direction **until the gap is violated** (filled).

| Gap type | Where | Meaning / identification |
|---|---|---|
| **Breakaway** | Exit from a trading range or congestion area (a range held for a few weeks) | Starts a new move; needs strong imbalance; **volume increases** (GM example) |
| **Runaway / measuring** | Mid-trend | Late-comers who waited for a pullback give up and chase; "almost panic" buying; volume rises (Ford) |
| **Exhaustion** | Near the end of a strong trend | Often the **first signal the move is over**; very high volume and a big gap; easy to confuse with runaway gaps unless the exceptional volume is noticed (Delta Air Lines) |

## 10. Indicator analysis

- **Indicators** are mathematical treatments of price and volume over a period. They aim to predict where, and in which direction, price will move **in the near future** by relating current price to past prices.

### Moving averages (MA)
- The average of closing prices over a rolling window, recalculated each period.
- **Simple MA (SMA):** SMA_n = (C₁ + C₂ + … + C_n) / n.
- Pullback strategy (20-day SMA on DIA):
  1. An uptrend is established.
  2. Price dips below the MA.
  3. **Buy when price closes back above the MA.**
  - The strategy fails once the trend breaks; note the crash on the right of that chart.

### Crossovers
- A crossover is the point where price and an indicator, or two indicators, intersect. It is used to forecast moves.
- **Golden cross:** buy when the **50-day SMA crosses above the 200-day SMA**.
- **Death cross:** sell when the 50 crosses **below** the 200 (SPY example).
- These signals lag and can **whipsaw** in sideways markets, as the SPY chart shows (sell then buy shortly after).

### Relative Strength Index (RSI)
- Developed by **J. Welles Wilder**. A momentum oscillator measuring the **speed and change** of price moves.
- **Formula:**
  - **RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + RS)**
  - **RS = average of x periods' up-closes ÷ average of x periods' down-closes** (usually x = 14).
- Range 0–100: **above 70 = overbought**; **below 30 = oversold**.
- GBP/USD daily example (2010): RSI dipped under 30 near the May low and rose above 70 near the August high, both close to turning points.

## 11. Weaknesses of technical analysis

- **Needs experience** to identify and interpret patterns correctly.
- **Analyst bias**: you tend to see what you want to see.
- Patterns must be **spotted quickly**; late recognition means a poor entry.
- The deck says **emphasis should be on long-term patterns**, because short-term moves are noisier.
- **Unsuitable for new listings**, which have no price history.
- **Cannot forecast new, unprecedented events** such as the 2008 financial crisis.

## 12. Practical takeaways for a short-term trader

1. **Trade with the higher-timeframe trend.** Use Dow's hierarchy: identify the primary trend (daily/weekly) and take short-term signals only in its direction. Minor trends are mostly noise.
2. **Mark support and resistance before the session.** Range trading (buy support, sell resistance) works only while the range holds. A break flips the logic (resistance becomes support).
3. **Wait for confirmation.** Act on neckline breaks, a **candle close** beyond the level, and ideally a volume spike. Do not act on half-formed patterns. In FX, use tick volume or volatility as a proxy.
4. **Define entry, stop and target in advance** using the pattern's measured move (pattern height or flagpole length). Skip trades without a sensible reward-to-risk ratio.
5. **Combine a trend filter with an oscillator.** For example, trade only in the direction of the 50/200 MA alignment and use RSI pullbacks (e.g. RSI near 40–50 in an uptrend, or under 30 in a range) for timing. In strong trends RSI can stay overbought or oversold for a long time.
6. **MAs and crossovers lag and whipsaw in ranges.** Reduce size or stand aside when price chops around a flat MA.
7. **Treat gaps carefully.** In FX, gaps appear mainly at the weekly open and around news. A big gap on exceptional activity late in a trend may be exhaustion, not continuation.
8. **Watch for the "greater fool" trap.** Chasing parabolic moves with no basis is betting someone will pay more. Late entries into extended moves carry poor odds.
9. **TA cannot price in shocks.** News and central-bank events can override any pattern, so check the economic calendar and avoid holding short expiries through major releases.
10. **Patterns are subjective.** Keep a log of the patterns you trade and their real-world outcomes, so you know which ones actually work for your instrument and timeframe.
