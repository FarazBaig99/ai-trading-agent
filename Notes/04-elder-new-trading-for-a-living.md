# Alexander Elder — *The New Trading for a Living* — Study Notes

> **Source caveat (read first).** The file `Books/The New Trading for a Living PDF.pdf` is **not the full book**. It is a ~25,000-word *Bookey* summary of Elder's companion **Study Guide**, which is mostly quiz questions, short chapter summaries, Q&A and quotes. It names the key tools (EMAs, MACD-Histogram, Force Index, Impulse, channels, Stochastic, RSI, ADX, NH-NL, 2% / 6% rules, Iron Triangle, Trade Apgar, Tradebill, trade grades) but gives few exact settings or formulas.
> To make these notes usable, I added Elder's standard published settings and rules from the full book. Those additions are marked **[book]**. Everything else comes from the PDF. Treat the **[book]** items as reference values to check on your own charts, as Elder himself advises.

---

## 0. Core philosophy and the game you are playing

- **You must beat the crowd.** Trading is a **zero-sum game before costs and a minus-sum game after them.** Winners are paid out of losers' accounts, and brokers and exchanges take a cut from both sides. The majority has to lose.
- **Three pillars, the "3 Ms":**
  1. **Mind:** sound individual psychology and discipline.
  2. **Method:** a good analytic method and trading system.
  3. **Money:** careful money and risk management.
  - **[book]** Elder adds a 4th element in the new edition: **record-keeping (Management)**, which ties the other three together. The Study Guide calls good record-keeping "the most important skill for your growth."
- **Inside connections are not needed.** A knowledgeable trader can act as his own insider. Tips arrive late and never come with an exit, so use them only if they fit your own system. Trading on inside information is illegal in many countries for stocks, though not necessarily in futures.
- **Benign skepticism:** don't swallow any book whole, including Elder's. **Test every idea on your own data**, start small and increase size as competence grows.
- **The playing field tilts against you.** Slippage and commissions push the odds against most traders, and emotional trading finishes them off.
  - Worked example from the Study Guide: buy 100 shares at $20 with a market order filled at $20.08, so slippage is 8c × 100 = $8, almost as much as the commission. Across the round trip, commissions plus slippage took **$40, which was 50% of the gross profit**, and taxes still had to be paid.
  - **Ways to cut costs:** be very selective (fewer, better trades), negotiate lower commissions, use **limit orders to enter**, and use **market-if-touched (MIT) or stop orders to exit** quickly. Slippage is lower in liquid markets and on quiet, narrow-range days. A **50% margin** account must earn more than 2× the cost percentage just to break even.

## 1. MIND — Individual Psychology

### 1.1 Emotions are the main danger
- The greatest threat to your account is **you**. Fear and greed hurt equity more than a brilliant system helps it. When your mind is clouded, the best system gets thrown out the window.
- A **professional is calm and businesslike**. He knows ahead of time what he will do whether the market rises, falls or goes sideways. On unclear days he stands aside and watches.
- You don't need to be smarter than other traders. You need **better discipline**.
- **Trade the way you live.** Disorder, procrastination and self-sabotage in daily life show up in your trading. Problems that seem to come from outside often reflect inner conflicts.
- **Accept full responsibility.** Indicators, analysts and advisors can inform you, but only you decide when a trade starts and when it ends. Blaming brokers, news or "manipulators" keeps a loser stuck.

### 1.2 Risk attitude and goals
- Trading is risky by nature, since there is no reward without risk of loss. **Winners measure risk in advance and trade only when the odds favor them.** They don't avoid risk and they don't crave it.
- **Aim for your personal best, not for money.** A surgeon concentrates on the operation, not on the fee. Counting money in open trades leads to emotional decisions.
- **After a success,** stay grounded. Don't raise risk because you feel invincible.
- **During a losing streak,** don't hunt for a new magic system. Stop or cut size, review every trade, find the causes and fix them.

### 1.3 Gambling and self-destruction
- **Signs of gambling:** an irresistible urge to trade, inability to stop during bad conditions, reckless doubling up after losses, the "high" of action, hiding losses.
- **Trading for entertainment** or to feel powerful is gambling.
- **Losers resemble alcoholics.** Both hide the true size of the problem from themselves and others, chase recovery compulsively and look for a quick fix.

### 1.4 The recovery path ("Losers Anonymous" approach)
- **Step 1 is to admit "I am a loser,"** meaning a person prone to losing who needs a new way of operating.
- Once you accept that, you can **cut losses short**, since each small loss is just the cost of doing business rather than a blow to your ego.
- **[book]** Use a businesslike risk definition: decide in writing, before entry, the maximum loss that is "normal business expense." Anything beyond it is a "bad loss," a relapse.
- **Written trade plans** are the strongest single defense against emotional decisions. Study Guide answer: "the most important factor in becoming a successful trader is using written trading plans and avoiding emotional decisions."

### 1.5 Account size and systems
- **A large account helps** because it lets you diversify across markets and systems, and expenses become a smaller percentage. It is not mainly about being able to absorb bigger losses. Beginners with big accounts often waste the advantage through recklessness.
- **Commercial or black-box systems** fail because markets change and the buyer doesn't understand the system well enough to adjust it. Demand a **validated real-time track record**. It is better to build your own.
- If markets still seem mysterious after a year, the likely causes are **impulsive trading, too little study, or too small an account**.

## 2. MIND (continued) — Mass Psychology and Crowd Behavior

### 2.1 What price is
- **Each price is a momentary consensus of value** among all participants at the moment of the trade. **A moving average is the average consensus of value** over its window.
- In the long run, fundamentals anchor prices. **Day to day and over intermediate terms, crowd psychology rules.**
- **Three groups:** bulls (buyers who want to pay as little as possible), bears (sellers who want as much as possible) and **undecided traders**. The undecided add urgency because their potential entry forces bulls and bears to act quickly. That creates the "pressure" behind trends.

### 2.2 Crowds
- When a person joins a crowd, he becomes **more impulsive, emotional and primitive**. He gives up independent judgment and seeks reassurance from the group and its leaders, especially when uncertain.
- **Crowds are powerful but stupid.** Don't fight a trend, because the crowd is bigger than you. Don't marry it either: crowds are wrong at extremes ("market crowds are almost always wrong" at turning points).
- **Your job is to identify which group dominates and trade with it.** You are not forecasting. A good technician measures the **balance of power between bulls and bears** and bets on the winning side.
- **Trends are crowd behavior.** A trend persists while new losers keep feeding it. It ends when the dominant group runs out of fuel.
- A **price shock**, a sudden move against the trend, frightens the dominant group and emboldens the opposition. It is often an early warning.
- **Trade defensively.** Others are trying to pick your pocket before you pick theirs.
- **"When in doubt, stay out."** When unsure, trade smaller or stand aside. Don't ask gurus or the crowd.
- **Divergences show crowd shifts.** Bearish divergences mark tops of uptrends, where bulls weaken while prices still rise. Bullish divergences mark bottoms of downtrends.

### 2.3 Sentiment and consensus (contrarian measures)
- **Bullish consensus** (advisors or traders): in a strong uptrend it can reach **~75%**. In futures every long is matched by a short, so 75% bulls means the average bear holds three times the position of the average bull. That bear is the big, well-capitalized "smart money." **Bet on the wealthy minority.** A record bullish consensus warns of a top, so take long profits and look for shorts. **~20–25% bulls** warns of a bottom.
- "When everyone thinks alike, everyone is likely to be wrong."
- A **bull market that draws heavy public and media attention is old.** No bell rings at the start of a new bull. After about 11 months a bear market is old, so start accumulating slowly once price confirms.
- **Insider buying** (corporate insiders) is a bullish clue, but wait for price confirmation.
- **Commitments of Traders / position limits:** hedgers ("commercials") are exempt from speculative position limits. Large-speculator and reporting thresholds show what big players are doing.

## 3. METHOD — Classical Chart Analysis

- Technical analysis is **universal**. If you have open, high, low, close, volume (and open interest), you can judge the bull/bear balance in any market: stocks, bonds, forex, futures.
- **Patterns are easy to spot in the middle of a chart and hard at the right edge**, where you actually have to decide.
- Analysts disagree mostly because of **wishful thinking, fuzzy definitions and different timeframes.** A market can trend up on the daily and down on the weekly at the same time.
- **Amateurs vs pros:** amateurs read news overnight and dominate the **open**. Professionals work all day and dominate the **close**. So the open reflects amateur opinion and the close reflects professional opinion. The daily bar records a battle in which the bulls win if the close is near the high and the bears win if it is near the low.

### 3.1 Support and resistance
- Mark them at **peaks and troughs**, ideally across congestion zones rather than single extremes.
- **They exist because traders have memories,** including regret and pain at prior levels.
- **Strength grows with** the number of touches, the time spent in the zone, the **volume** traded there (financial and emotional commitment) and recency.
- **Role reversal:** broken resistance becomes support and broken support becomes resistance.
- Failure to rally up to prior resistance means weakness. Holding above support means strength.

### 3.2 Breakouts, false breakouts, trends and ranges
- **Before shorting a breakdown, wait for a new low** to confirm the downside break. Mirror this for longs.
- **False breakouts are among the best signals in technical analysis.** When price pokes out of a range and snaps back, trade back in the opposite direction toward the other side of the range. Amateurs get trapped while pros fade them.
- **Uptrend definition:** higher highs and higher lows. "The trend is your friend." Hold with the trend and **raise stops as it progresses.** Ways to enter an uptrend: buy breakouts to new highs (riskier), buy pullbacks to support or value (better), and protect earlier positions with stops.
- **Trading ranges** call for more caution and faster profit-taking, since reversals are frequent.

### 3.3 Kangaroo tails
- A **single very long bar sticking out of a tight congestion area**, much longer than its neighbors, followed by a return into the range. It shows that the market tested and **rejected** that price.
- **Trade against the tail:** a tail pointing down calls for buying and a tail pointing up calls for selling or shorting.
- **[book]** Place the protective stop about **halfway back along the tail**. Putting it at the very tip gives up too much, but some room is needed. The PDF's quiz wording on this point is muddled, so test it yourself.

## 4. METHOD — Computerized Technical Analysis (Indicators)

### 4.1 General principles
- **Computers make analysis more objective**, since an indicator is clearly up, down or flat, unlike arguments over trendlines. They **don't remove emotions**, because you still place the orders. Buying the best car doesn't make a beginner an expert driver.
- **Software types:** *Toolbox* (charting tools and indicators you combine yourself, which is what Elder prefers), *Gray box* (signals with adjustable parameters) and *Black box* (fixed, secret signals, which tend to fail when markets change).
- **Three indicator groups:**
  1. **Trend-following** (moving averages, MACD lines, Directional system): work in trends and whipsaw in ranges.
  2. **Oscillators** (MACD-Histogram, Force Index, Stochastic, RSI, Elder-ray, Williams %R): catch turning points in ranges, but give premature signals in trends.
  3. **Miscellaneous** (NH-NL, A/D, sentiment and similar): measure crowd mood.
- **Combine indicators from different groups** so each one's weaknesses cancel out and their strengths remain. That is the logic of Triple Screen.
- **"Trader, know thyself":** decide whether you are a trend-follower or a reversal-catcher, and build on that strength.

### 4.2 Moving averages (EMAs)
- **EMA vs SMA:** an EMA gives more weight to recent data and reacts faster. It also doesn't jump when an old price drops out of the window ("barks twice"). The one thing an SMA does better is that it is easier to calculate by hand.
- **[book] EMA formula:** EMA_today = Price_today × K + EMA_yesterday × (1 − K), where **K = 2 / (N + 1)**.
- **[book] Typical lengths:** **13-bar (fast)** and **26-bar (slow)** EMAs on both daily and weekly charts. The **22-day EMA** is used as the centerline for daily channels and for SafeZone trend direction. The 26-week EMA is roughly a 6-month trend.
- **Rule: the slope of the EMA is its key message.**
  - Rising EMA means **bulls are in control**, so trade long only and buy pullbacks toward the EMA.
  - Falling EMA means **bears are in control**, so trade short only and sell rallies toward the EMA.
- **[book] Value zone:** the space **between the fast and slow EMA**. In an uptrend, buy pullbacks into value. Prices far above value are overextended and are a place to take profits, not to buy.
- **[book] Average-penetration entry:** measure how far prices typically dip below the fast EMA during recent pullbacks in an uptrend. Place a buy limit at (tomorrow's projected EMA − average penetration). Shorts mirror this. The PDF's version: "Don't be greedy trying to nail the bottom; catching a $1 penetration is more reliable than a $3 one."

### 4.3 MACD and MACD-Histogram
- **[book] Settings:** **12-, 26- and 9-bar EMAs** of closes.
  - **Fast MACD line** = 12 EMA − 26 EMA.
  - **Slow (signal) line** = 9 EMA of the MACD line.
  - **MACD-Histogram** = MACD line − signal line, plotted as bars around zero.
- **MACD lines (trend-following):** fast line crossing above slow line is bullish, and crossing below is bearish. These are slower, less frequent signals.
- **MACD-Histogram (oscillator, Elder's favorite):** it shows the **spread between the lines** and therefore whether bulls or bears are gaining strength.
  - **The slope between the last two bars matters most.** A rising slope (current bar higher than the previous one, even below zero) means bulls are gaining, and a falling slope means bears are gaining. "Uptick" and "downtick" are the actionable messages.
  - **Record new high in MACD-H** (e.g., highest in ~3 months on daily) means bulls are very strong, so prices are likely to retest or exceed the recent peak. **Record new low** means bears are strong and the recent low is likely to be retested or broken.
  - **Weekly MACD-H slope** is one option for Triple Screen's first screen: an uptick allows only longs and a downtick allows only shorts.
- **Divergences (the strongest signals in technical analysis):**
  - **Bullish divergence:** price makes a **new low** but MACD-H makes a **shallower bottom**. **[book] Valid only if MACD-H crosses above zero between the two bottoms** ("breaking the back of the bear"). The buy signal comes when MACD-H **ticks up from the second, shallower bottom**. Put the stop below the latest price low.
  - **Bearish divergence:** price makes a **new high** but MACD-H makes a **lower peak**, with a **[book]** dip below zero between the peaks. The sell or short signal comes when the second peak ticks down. Put the stop above the latest high.
  - Divergences on **weekly** charts carry more weight than daily ones. "Missing right shoulder" variants exist, where the indicator fails to rise above zero on the second wave.

### 4.4 Directional System (DMI / ADX)
- **[book] Settings:** 13-bar smoothing. **+DI** shows upward movement and **−DI** shows downward movement. **ADX** measures trend strength regardless of direction.
- **+DI above −DI** means bulls lead. **−DI above +DI** means bears lead.
- **Rising ADX** means the trend is strengthening, so use trend-following methods. A rise from below both DI lines is the best early signal.
- **ADX low or falling, below both DI lines,** means a trendless market. Avoid trend-following methods there, since oscillators work better.
- **ADX above both DI lines then turning down** means the trend is overheated, so **take partial profits** and expect a possible reversal or higher volatility.

### 4.5 Oscillators: overbought and oversold
- Oscillators mark **extremes of crowd sentiment**. Overbought means too bullish and likely to fall. Oversold means too bearish and likely to rise.
- **They work in ranges and mislead in strong trends.** In a strong uptrend an oscillator can stay overbought for a long time. Use them in the direction of the higher-timeframe trend, buying oversold readings in uptrends only.
- **Divergences on oscillators are their best signals.**

### 4.6 Stochastic
- **[book] Settings:** 5-bar **%K**, smoothed with 3-bar **%D** (slow stochastic). Reference lines at **80 / 20** (some use 85/15 or 70/30).
- **[book] Formula:** %K = (Close − Lowest Low_n) / (Highest High_n − Lowest Low_n) × 100.
- **Rules:**
  - In a weekly **uptrend**, a daily Stochastic **below ~30** (oversold) is a buy setup.
  - In a weekly **downtrend**, a daily Stochastic **above ~70** (overbought) is a short setup.
  - A **reading of 85** in a weekly uptrend is **not** a short signal. Stand aside or take profits on longs, but don't short against the tide.
  - **Divergences** between price and Stochastic, especially a %K/%D crossover near the reference lines after a divergence, are the strongest signals.

### 4.7 RSI (Relative Strength Index)
- **[book] Settings:** Elder prefers **13 bars** (Wilder used 14, and 7 is more sensitive). Reference lines at **70 / 30**. Draw them so RSI crosses them only at true extremes, about 5% of the time.
- **[book] Formula:** RSI = 100 − 100 / (1 + RS), where RS = average up-close move / average down-close move over n bars.
- **Rules:**
  - **Bullish divergence:** price makes a new low and RSI makes a higher low, often from below 30. Buy when RSI turns up. **Bearish divergence:** price makes a new high and RSI makes a lower high. Sell when RSI turns down.
  - **Overbought / oversold levels** are useful mostly in ranges.
  - Trendlines and chart patterns drawn on RSI often break before price does.

### 4.8 Elder-ray (Bull Power / Bear Power)
- The Study Guide summary mentions it only in passing. **[book] Definition:**
  - Centerline: **13-bar EMA** (consensus of value).
  - **Bull Power = High − 13 EMA**, which measures how far bulls can push above value.
  - **Bear Power = Low − 13 EMA**, which measures how far bears can push below value.
- **[book] Buy rules (EMA rising is required):**
  - Bear Power is **negative but rising** (ticking up), meaning bears are weakening during a pullback. This is the essential condition.
  - The best extra condition is a **bullish divergence in Bear Power**.
  - Don't buy when Bear Power is positive, because there is no pullback and the market is overextended.
- **[book] Short rules (EMA falling is required):**
  - Bull Power is **positive but falling**.
  - The best extra condition is a **bearish divergence in Bull Power**.
- Used as a Triple Screen **second-screen oscillator**.

### 4.9 The Impulse System
- **Purpose:** a **censorship system**. It doesn't tell you what to do, only what you are **forbidden** to do.
- **Inputs:** the **slope of the 13-bar EMA** (inertia or trend) and the **slope of MACD-Histogram (12-26-9)** (momentum or force).
- **Bar colors:**
  - **Green:** EMA up **and** MACD-H up, so bulls are pushing. **Shorting is forbidden.** Buy or stand aside.
  - **Red:** EMA down **and** MACD-H down, so bears are pushing. **Buying is forbidden.** Short or stand aside.
  - **Blue:** the two disagree, so the impulse is neutral. **Either direction is allowed.**
- **[book] Usage:**
  - Apply it on two timeframes. **Weekly Impulse** acts as a trend filter: do not buy when weekly is red, and do not short when weekly is green. **Daily Impulse** then times entries.
  - Enter long when daily turns from red to **not-red**, preferably in a pullback to value. Aggressive long exit: **take profits when daily turns from green to not-green** (blue), since the momentum burst is over.
  - Never buy into a red bar. Don't short into a green bar.

### 4.10 Channels and envelopes
- **Construction:** draw two lines **parallel to a moving average** (**[book]** the 22-day EMA on dailies; use a similar method on weeklies). Adjust the coefficient until the channel holds **about 95% of prices over the last ~100 bars**, so that only the extreme highs and lows poke out. Re-tune it periodically.
  - **[book]** Upper line = EMA + EMA × coefficient. Lower line = EMA − EMA × coefficient.
  - **[book]** An alternative is **ATR channels**: lines at EMA ± 1, 2 and 3 ATR. A move beyond 3 ATR is an extreme overshoot.
- **What it means:**
  - The **slope of the channel** shows the trend: rising is bullish and falling is bearish.
  - **The upper line** is the zone where bullishness turns manic, meaning overvalued. **The lower line** is where bearishness turns to panic, meaning undervalued.
  - Channel height measures volatility and sets the yardstick for grading performance.
- **Rules:**
  - **Don't buy above the upper channel line.** Bulls are manic there and reversals are more likely, even if the move continues.
  - **Swing target:** when buying near a rising EMA (value), the reasonable target is **the upper channel line**. For shorts near a falling EMA, target the **lower line**.
  - In an uptrend, expect prices to rise much farther above the EMA than they fall below it on pullbacks.
  - Price stuck near one line without reaching the other is a sign of trend strength.

### 4.11 Indicator "seasons"
- Use the **weekly MACD-H or another oscillator** and look at its position relative to zero plus its slope:
  - **Spring:** below zero and rising. This is **the best time to buy**.
  - **Summer:** above zero and rising. Still bullish, but late. Buy carefully.
  - **Autumn:** above zero and falling. This is **the best time to sell or short**.
  - **Winter:** below zero and falling. Bearish, but late for new shorts.

## 5. METHOD — Volume, Time and Open Interest

### 5.1 Volume
- **Every trade has a buyer and a seller, so volume is the activity of both winners and losers.** It reflects financial and emotional commitment and pain. Volume does **not** tell you how participants will react to future prices.
- **Rules:**
  - **Rising or steady volume confirms a trend.** In a healthy uptrend, volume is higher on rallies and lighter on pullbacks, and light-volume pullbacks are buying chances.
  - **A new price high or low on shrinking volume** means the trend is likely to pause or reverse.
  - **Trends end with a bang or a whimper**: a climactic volume spike (panic or mania, with losers bailing out) or a drastic drying-up of volume.
  - Volume spikes often come with panic selling near bottoms.
- **On-Balance Volume (OBV):** a running total that adds volume on up-close days and subtracts it on down-close days. **New OBV lows confirm bearish moves.** OBV often **peaks before price peaks**, giving early warning. Divergences between OBV and price are signals.
- **Accumulation/Distribution [book, Williams version]:** A/D = ((Close − Open) / (High − Low)) × Volume, accumulated over time. It credits the bar's open-to-close move, so it compares amateurs at the open with professionals at the close.

### 5.2 Force Index (Elder's volume oscillator)
- **[book] Formula:** **Force Index = Volume × (Close_today − Close_yesterday)**. It combines **direction** (sign), **extent** (size of the change) and **volume**.
- **Smoothing:**
  - **2-bar EMA of Force Index (short-term):** a Triple Screen second-screen oscillator for timing entries.
    - **Buy** when it falls **below zero** (dips) during an uptrend (rising 13- or 22-EMA, or weekly uptrend).
    - **Short** when it rises **above zero** during a downtrend.
    - Bullish divergences on it near bottoms are strong buy signals.
  - **13-bar EMA of Force Index (longer-term):** a trend indicator.
    - **Crossing above zero is bullish** and **crossing below zero is bearish**, which flags trend reversals.
    - New highs of the 13-EMA Force Index confirm trends. Divergences warn of reversals.
- Study Guide logic: if the weekly trend is up but daily Force Index is down, that is the buy setup, and you place a buy order. If there is a **bearish divergence in daily Force Index while the weekly trend is up**, **stand aside**: don't buy against the warning and don't short against the weekly tide.

### 5.3 Open interest (futures and options)
- Open interest is the number of contracts outstanding, with longs always equal to shorts.
- It **rises** when new buyers and new sellers open positions, meaning confident bulls face confident bears. One side will lose, but **while new losers keep coming in, the trend continues.**
- It **falls** when positions are closed, because losers are leaving and the trend is losing fuel.

### 5.4 Time
- **All trading decisions must be analyzed in more than one timeframe.**
- **Factor of five:** adjacent timeframes relate by about ×5. Examples: monthly–weekly–daily, or daily–hourly–10-minute. Choose your favorite timeframe, go **one level up to make the strategic decision**, and use your own (or one level down) for tactics.
- Cycles reflect both fundamental factors (such as seasonal supply) and swings in crowd psychology between greed and fear.

## 6. METHOD — General Market (Stock Market) Indicators

- **New High–New Low Index (NH-NL):** stocks at 52-week highs minus stocks at 52-week lows each day. It is the **best leading indicator of the stock market**, because it measures the strength of leaders.
  - **[book]** Track a daily line, a 10-day sum (short term) and a 65-day sum (~a quarter, used on the weekly chart).
  - **Bullish zone:** above zero, ideally **above +100**. **Bearish zone:** below zero, ideally **below −100**.
  - **During a rally, a new NH-NL high** means bullish leadership is strong, so **trade from the long side**. **During a decline, a new NH-NL low** means bearish leadership is strong, so trade short.
  - **Divergences:** if the index rallies to a new high but NH-NL makes a lower peak, leadership is fading and the top is near. If the index falls to a new low but NH-NL makes a higher low, bears are weakening and a bottom is near.
- **Advance/Decline line:** a cumulative total of advancing minus declining issues, which measures **mass participation** by counting stocks rather than weighing volume. A rising A/D line confirms rallies, and a falling one says declines will continue. Watch whether its peaks and valleys confirm the index.
- **Sentiment indicators:** bullish consensus above ~75% points to a top and below ~25% to a bottom (see §2.3). Also watch insider buying and selling and Commitments of Traders.

## 7. METHOD — Trading Systems

### 7.1 What a system is
- A **set of rules for entries, targets and stops**, designed calmly when markets are closed. It is "an island of stability" in the emotional storm, not a money machine.
- **Testing:** backtest first, then **forward-test (paper trade), keeping records**. **[book]** Do a manual "one bar at a time" test: start at the beginning of the data and advance one day at a time, recording every signal, the planned action and the result. Then trade small with real money.
- **Before any trade, write three numbers: entry, target and stop.** "A trade without these numbers is not a trade but a gamble." Expected profit is useful for the reward/risk ratio, but the plan does not depend on a profit goal.

### 7.2 Triple Screen trading system, step by step
**Problem it solves:** trend-followers and oscillators contradict each other, and timeframes conflict (the trend can be up on the daily and down on the weekly at the same time). Triple Screen **uses the higher timeframe for strategy and the lower one for tactics**, with a different type of tool on each screen.

**Screen 1 — The market tide (long-term trend). Make the strategic decision on the higher timeframe (e.g., weekly).**
- Always look at the weekly chart **before** the daily, or the daily will bias you.
- **Tool: a trend-following indicator.** Classic version: **slope of weekly MACD-Histogram**. **[book]** New-edition preference: **slope of the weekly 26-week EMA** (or 13-week) and/or **weekly Impulse** (green or blue allows longs, red or blue allows shorts).
- **Rule:** weekly up means **only long or flat**. Weekly down means **only short or flat**. If the weekly chart is unclear, stand aside or use a different method.
- Study Guide: if weekly MACD-H slopes **down**, the only choices are **short or stand aside**. Never buy.

**Screen 2 — The market wave (intermediate counter-move). Find the setup on the trading timeframe (e.g., daily).**
- **Tool: an oscillator.** Use it to find **pullbacks against the weekly trend**.
  - Daily **Force Index (2-EMA)** below zero in a weekly uptrend is a buy setup. Above zero in a weekly downtrend is a short setup.
  - Daily **Stochastic** below ~30 in an uptrend means buy, and above ~70 in a downtrend means short.
  - **Elder-ray:** Bear Power negative and rising in an uptrend means buy. Bull Power positive and falling in a downtrend means short.
  - **[book]** Also usable: Williams %R and a daily Impulse turning from red to blue during a weekly uptrend.
- **Summary matrix:**

| Weekly trend (Screen 1) | Daily oscillator (Screen 2) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Up | Down (oversold, pullback) | **Prepare to buy** |
| Up | Up (overbought) | Stand aside or hold longs; don't short |
| Down | Up (overbought, rally) | **Prepare to short** |
| Down | Down (oversold) | Stand aside or hold shorts; don't buy |

- A weekly uptrend with a daily bearish divergence calls for standing aside.

**Screen 3 — The ripple (entry technique). Execute on intraday data or with orders.**
- **Trailing buy-stop (classic):** in a weekly uptrend with a daily pullback, place a **buy-stop 1 tick above the previous day's high**. If it isn't filled, **lower it the next day** to 1 tick above that day's high, and repeat until filled or until the weekly trend fails or the setup is invalid. The idea is to catch the moment the pullback ends and the upside resumes. Shorts mirror this: a sell-stop below the previous day's low, raised each day.
- **[book] Value entry (modern alternative):** a **limit order inside the value zone** (between the 13 and 26 EMA) or at **EMA − average penetration**. This is cheaper than buying breakouts and gives better trade grades.
- **Protective stop on entry:** **[book]** below the low of the entry day or the previous day, whichever is lower (shorts mirror this), or a SafeZone or Nic stop (see §9.3).
- **Target:** the upper channel line for swing trades, or support or resistance for longer trades (see §9.2).
- **Position size:** the 2% rule via the Iron Triangle, plus a 6% rule check (see §8).

### 7.3 Other system points
- **Channel trading:** in a rising channel, buy near the EMA or value and sell near the upper line. In a falling channel, short near the EMA and cover near the lower line.
- **Impulse** as a censor sits on top of any system (see §4.9).

## 8. MONEY — Risk Management

### 8.1 Priorities and principles
- "No system will do you any good if you don't manage risk." Risk rules are your **lifeboats**.
- **Goals of money management in order:** (1) **survival**, (2) a **steady rate of return**, (3) **high returns**. Never sacrifice #1 for #3.
- **Drawdown math:** a series of small losses can add up to a big drawdown, and recovering takes a disproportionately larger gain. **[book]** A 20% loss needs +25% to recover, and a 50% loss needs +100%. In the Study Guide example, Trader A goes broke after three losses because of oversized risk, while Trader B survives nine losses because he risked less per trade.
- **Two deadly errors:** (1) **trading without stops**; (2) **risking more than the account can afford** (oversized trades). Related sins include **averaging down** (adding to losers is denial and hope, not analysis) and getting **margin calls**.
- **Why traders hold losers:** emotional attachment, refusal to admit being wrong, and unwillingness to give up hope. **Reluctance to take a loss is the mark of emotional trading.**
- **Don't count money in open trades.** If you catch yourself thinking about what a paper profit will buy, close the trade.
- **Trade size swings:** don't double size because you feel elated after a win, and don't revenge-trade after a loss.

### 8.2 The 2% Rule (shark-bite protection)
- **Never risk more than 2% of account equity on any single trade.** Risk means the distance from **entry to stop × shares**, plus commissions and slippage. **[book]** Many professionals risk less, 0.25–1%. Beginners with small accounts should treat 2% as the absolute ceiling, not a target. Risking 10% per trade is never acceptable.
- Compute it from **equity at the start of the month** (account value), not from margin.

### 8.3 The 6% Rule (piranha protection)
- **[book] Exact rule:** **stop opening new trades for the rest of the calendar month when this month's realized losses plus the risk in all open trades reach 6% of the account equity at the start of the month.** Open trades that are protected at breakeven or better count as zero risk.
- **Why:** it limits monthly drawdown. A bad month ends early instead of becoming a disaster, and it forces a pause to review whether the system or the trader's state is at fault.
- **Corollary:** it caps how many trades you can have open at once. With 2% risk each, you can have about three fresh full-risk trades open. To add more, move existing stops to breakeven, which frees "risk budget."
- **Scaling up:** grow size only after **profitable months**, typically in steps, and recompute 2% and 6% from new equity each month.

### 8.4 Position sizing: the Iron Triangle
- Three corners: **(1) maximum $ risk per trade** (from the 2% and 6% rules), **(2) entry price**, **(3) stop price**.
- **Formula:**
  - **Max $ risk = Account equity × 2%** (or your lower percentage).
  - **Risk per share/contract = |Entry − Stop|**.
  - **Position size (shares) = Max $ risk ÷ Risk per share.** Round down, and include commissions and slippage.
- **Worked example:** a $50,000 account allows $1,000 risk at 2%. Buy at $40 with a stop at $38, so $2 risk per share and 500 shares max. Check the 6% rule as well, because if the open risk elsewhere plus this month's losses is already 5%, only $500 is allowed, which is 250 shares.
- **Futures/forex:** risk per contract = stop distance × point value. Contracts = max $ risk ÷ risk per contract. If even 1 contract exceeds 2%, skip the trade.

### 8.5 Stops management
- **Place the stop the moment you enter.** Adjust it **only in the direction of the trade.** Raise it for longs and lower it for shorts, and never widen it.
- **Hard stop vs soft stop [book]:** a hard stop is an actual order in the market. A soft (mental) stop is acted on manually, usually near the close. Soft stops are only for experienced, disciplined traders. Beginners should use hard stops.
- Stops are generally reliable but **don't guarantee the maximum loss**, because prices can **gap** through them. That is one more reason to keep size small.
- After a profitable move, **move the stop to breakeven** at a pre-planned level written in the Tradebill. Then trail it to protect part of the paper profit.
- **The protecting-profit cost example:** if you raise the stop to lock in profit and get stopped out, costs (commission plus slippage) can eat a large share of the small gross profit, as in the 50% example. Factor this in.

### 8.6 After a drawdown
- **Losses traumatize. Be gentle with yourself.** Take a break, return **at reduced size** with a lower risk per trade (e.g., 0.5–1%), aim for modest goals, and **step size back up gradually** after a run of profitable trades or months.
- Temporarily tighten the 2% and 6% limits during recovery.

## 9. Practical Details — Entries, Targets, Stops, Trade Grading, Scans

### 9.1 Entries
- **At the right edge you must decide now: buy, sell or stand aside.** Standing aside is a **legitimate** choice and often the best one.
- **Entry methods, from aggressive to conservative:**
  1. **Breakout buy** above the previous day's high or a resistance level. This fits strong bullish conviction, but it carries more slippage and false-breakout risk, and it is **not always safe**.
  2. **Pullback to value:** a limit order between the fast and slow EMAs. This is **the preferred method** in an uptrend.
  3. **Dip below value / average penetration:** buy at EMA − average downside penetration, within prior pullback zones.
- **Never buy above the upper channel line.** Never chase. Don't try to catch the exact bottom ("catching a falling knife").

### 9.2 Profit targets
- **Swing trades:** after buying near a rising EMA, target the **upper channel line**. After shorting near a falling EMA, target the **lower channel line**, or the **value zone** when shorting from above the channel.
- **Long-term or position trades:** don't use the EMA or channel, which are too sensitive and cause premature exits. Use **support and resistance**, major prior highs and lows, weekly channels and **divergences** for targets and exits.
- Write the target **before** entry so you can compute **reward/risk**. **[book]** Skip trades with a poor ratio, e.g., below ~2:1.

### 9.3 Stops: placement methods
- **Principles:**
  - **Place the stop outside the zone of market noise**, where random fluctuation won't hit it. This reduces **whipsaws**. Stops themselves don't cause whipsaws; bad placement does.
  - **Avoid obvious levels**: round numbers and exact, highly visible prior extremes, because too many stops cluster there and get hunted. Put the stop **slightly beyond** the obvious low or high, or a little **closer** inside it (the Nic stop).
  - A stop based on **support or resistance** is an effective noise-avoidance method.
  - Move stops to protect gains and never let a good winner turn into a loser.
- **Specific stop methods [book]:**
  - **Previous-bar stop:** below the lower of today's and yesterday's low (longs).
  - **SafeZone stop:**
    1. Find the trend from the slope of the **22-day EMA**.
    2. In an **uptrend**, over a lookback of about **10–20 bars**, record every **downside penetration** (the amount by which a day's low falls below the previous day's low).
    3. Compute the **average downside penetration** (sum ÷ number of penetrations).
    4. **Multiply by a coefficient, usually 2–3.**
    5. **Stop = yesterday's low − (coefficient × average penetration).**
    6. **Never lower the stop**, only raise it.
    7. For **downtrends**, use upside penetrations above previous highs: stop = yesterday's high + (coefficient × average penetration), and never raise it.
  - **Nic stop:** place the stop below the **less extreme of the two most recent lows** near the entry, not below the very lowest point, so you avoid the crowd's stops while keeping risk tight.
  - **Volatility-drop / ATR stops:** e.g., 1–2 ATR beyond the entry, or beyond the EMA.
  - **Time stop:** exit if the trade doesn't move as expected within a set number of bars.
- **Kangaroo tail trades:** stop about halfway back along the tail.

### 9.4 Trade grading (performance measurement)
- **Buy grade** = (High of the bar − Buy price) / (High − Low) of the entry bar. Buying at the bar's midpoint scores **50%**. Higher is better, since you want to buy near the low.
- **Sell grade** = (Sell price − Low of the bar) / (High − Low) of the exit bar. Selling near the high is best.
- **Overall trade grade (most important)** = **points captured ÷ channel height** at entry (daily channel). In other words, what share of the channel's swing you captured. **[book]** Ratings:
  - **A: 30% or more**
  - **B: 20–30%**
  - **C: 10–20%**
  - **D: under 10%** or a loss
  - A 30% capture is a strong performance.

### 9.5 Scanning
- A scan finds **candidates**, not profits. First **define the pattern** you trade, then cast the net as wide or narrow as you like.
- Use **negative filters** to exclude stocks that are unprofitable, in downtrends (for long scans), or that have **insufficient volume** or liquidity.

### 9.6 Choosing trading vehicles
- **Key factors: liquidity** (low slippage), **volatility** (opportunity) and **time zones** (the market should be open when you are alert). **Popularity is not a factor.** Unpopular markets can be excellent if liquid and volatile.
- **Stocks** make you a part-owner, while **bonds** are loans. Good company profits don't guarantee a rising stock, since price depends on expectations.
- **Mr. Market (Buffett):** he is manic-depressive. **Buy when he is depressed and sell when he is happy.**
- **ETFs:** baskets traded like stocks. Expense ratios create drag ("haircuts"), and price can drift from the underlying assets.
- **Options:** calls bet on a rise and puts on a fall. They lose time value approaching expiry, and higher volatility means higher premiums. **A buyer wins in only 1 of 3 scenarios** (up, down or flat) and must be right on direction, size and timing, while writers win in several. Option buyers risk only the premium, whereas futures risk is open-ended. Writers need stops.
- **Futures:** the biggest danger is **tiny margins (~5%)**, which mean huge leverage. Contracts are short-lived. **Forex** works the same way: know the key factors of whatever you trade.

## 10. Record Keeping — Trading Diary, Tradebill, Trade Apgar

### 10.1 Why
- Good records **identify mistakes so you stop repeating them** ("don't step on the same rake twice"), **show what produced profits so you can repeat it**, and **tie together psychology, analysis and risk management.**
- They bring **greater discipline and less impulsivity**, and they make you your own teacher. Records don't prevent mistakes, but they let you understand and correct them.

### 10.2 Daily routine
- **Morning homework:** it wakes you up and focuses you. Review overnight markets and scheduled reports (earnings, economic data), look over yesterday's trades and **adjust stops**, and focus on what matters **today**.
- **Daily psychological self-test:** rate your **health, mood and homework completion** before trading. **Don't trade** when sick, low or unprepared.
  - **Don't increase size on days when everything feels perfect.** From perfect, any change can only be for the worse.

### 10.3 Trade plan and Trade Apgar
- **Trade plan contents:** strategy, entry, target, stop and **$ risk** (so share size follows from it). Profit hopes are not part of the plan. Focus on **trade quality** until the trade is closed.
- **Trade Apgar:** named after the newborn Apgar score. It is a **quick 5-question screen with 3 possible answers each (scored 0/1/2)**, used to decide whether a stock **deserves a full trade plan**.
  - **[book]** Typical questions for a long:
    1. **Weekly Impulse:** red = 0, green = 1, blue = 2. Blue is best after a decline, since a turn is starting.
    2. **Daily Impulse:** scored the same way.
    3. **Price vs value / daily channel:** above value = 0, in value = 1, below value = 2.
    4. **False breakout:** none = 0, a false downside breakout = 2.
    5. **Perfection:** weekly and daily signals agree.
  - **[book]** Take the trade only with a high total, e.g., **7 or more and no zeros** on key items.

### 10.4 Tradebill (one per trade)
- **Before entry it records:**
  - strategy or system name
  - entry, target and stop (soft and hard)
  - **$ risk allotted** and the resulting **position size** (Iron Triangle)
  - reward/risk ratio
  - **upcoming earnings or report dates**, plus any reminders
- **After entry it records:**
  - the actual fill
  - **where the stop sits** (hard/soft)
  - **the price at which to move the stop to breakeven**
  - **the targeted profit**
  - confirmation that the trade has been entered in the **diary**

### 10.5 Trading diary / journal and spreadsheet
- **[book] Trading spreadsheet:** one row per trade with date, symbol, long/short, size, entry, exit, commissions, P/L, buy grade, sell grade and trade grade. Also keep an **equity curve** and a **monthly risk ledger** for the 6% Rule.
- **Trading diary:** for every trade, save **entry charts** (weekly and daily, annotated with reasons) and **exit charts**. Note your emotions and mistakes.
- **Follow-up review:** **weeks or months after exit, revisit each trade** with an updated chart. Trends and reversals are clear by then, so you can see exactly what you did right and wrong. **The diary is the most educational record.**
- **Reviewing closed trades:** focus on current trades during the day, but study closed ones systematically for lessons without dwelling on them.

## 11. Checklists

### 11.1 Pre-trading-day checklist
- [ ] Self-test: health, mood and sleep are OK? If not, **don't trade** or trade tiny.
- [ ] Morning homework done: overnight markets, news, reports and earnings dates for open or candidate positions?
- [ ] Yesterday's trades reviewed and **stops adjusted (only in the trade's direction)**?
- [ ] 6% check: this month's realized losses plus open risk are below 6% of start-of-month equity?

### 11.2 Pre-trade checklist (Triple Screen long; mirror it for shorts)
- [ ] **Screen 1:** is the weekly trend up (weekly EMA rising and/or weekly MACD-H rising, weekly Impulse **not red**)?
- [ ] **Screen 2:** a daily pullback is present (Force Index 2-EMA < 0, Stochastic < 30, Bear Power negative but rising, daily Impulse not red or turning blue)?
- [ ] No bearish divergence on daily or weekly indicators? Bonus if there is a **bullish divergence** (MACD-H, Force Index, Elder-ray, RSI).
- [ ] Price is **at or below value** (between the 13 and 26 EMA), **not above the upper channel line**?
- [ ] Trade Apgar score acceptable?
- [ ] **Entry** chosen: buy-stop above the previous high (trailing), or limit order at value or average penetration?
- [ ] **Stop** chosen outside the noise and away from round or obvious levels (SafeZone, Nic stop, previous-bar low)?
- [ ] **Target** chosen: upper channel line (swing) or resistance (position)? Reward/risk acceptable?
- [ ] **Size** = (2% × equity, or less) ÷ (entry − stop), adjusted down if the 6% budget is tight?
- [ ] Liquidity OK, and no earnings or report right before or after entry unless planned?
- [ ] **Tradebill filled in** and the trade written in the diary with an annotated entry chart?

### 11.3 In-trade management checklist
- [ ] Hard stop placed **immediately** on entry?
- [ ] Move the stop to breakeven at the pre-written level, then trail it (SafeZone and similar), never against the trade?
- [ ] Take profits at the target, **or earlier** if the daily Impulse turns not-green (aggressive), price reaches the upper channel line, or a bearish divergence appears?
- [ ] Not thinking about money? If you are fixated on P/L, close the trade.
- [ ] No averaging down, ever?

### 11.4 Post-trade checklist
- [ ] Record the exit, P/L and costs in the spreadsheet?
- [ ] Calculate the **buy grade, sell grade and trade grade** (% of channel captured)?
- [ ] Save the exit chart to the diary with notes on mistakes and emotions?
- [ ] Update the equity curve and the monthly 6% ledger?
- [ ] Schedule a **follow-up review** weeks or months later?

### 11.5 Recovery checklist (after a drawdown or 6% stop-out)
- [ ] Stop trading for the rest of the month (6% rule). Review every losing trade for its cause.
- [ ] Ask whether the problem is the system (market changed) or the trader (discipline, emotions, oversize).
- [ ] Return with **reduced size and a lower risk %**, paper-trade if needed, and increase size only after consistent profits.

## 12. Key one-liners to remember
- The trend is your friend, but the crowd is wrong at extremes.
- Trade with the dominant group and measure the bull/bear balance rather than forecasting.
- Decide with the weekly chart, act on the daily, and enter with intraday orders.
- The EMA slope gives the trend, MACD-H slope gives momentum, and both together give the Impulse.
- Divergences are the strongest signals: the histogram must cross zero between the two bottoms or peaks.
- Buy value and sell mania: never buy above the upper channel line.
- Three numbers before every trade: entry, target, stop. Without them it's a gamble.
- 2% per trade and 6% per month. Survival first.
- Place the stop at entry and only ever move it in the trade's direction.
- When in doubt, stay out. Standing aside is a position.
- Records turn experience into skill. Don't step on the same rake twice.
